# AetherFire Anti-Drift — Mortality, Relationship & Plot-Convenience Immunity v1.0

> **Type:** simulation-control / narrative-causality anti-drift overlay  
> **Applies to:** Total War RP, political conflict, faction conflict, war simulation, high-risk operations, actor interaction, romance, dynastic relations, protagonist networks, succession, betrayal, alliance, captivity, injury, and death.  
> **Not:** canon, mortality table, death generator, random-kill system, character arc planner, war-outcome generator, or permission to kill actors for drama.  
> **Authority hook:** inherits source authority, truth-status discipline, UNKNOWN handling, typed-relation discipline, and simulation rules from the active AetherFire CI, Source Router, and `AetherFire_Anti_Drift_Total_War_RP_v1.1.md`.  
> **Core purpose:** prevent actor survival, rescue, escape, immunity, or delayed consequences from being justified by narrative importance, relationship importance, protagonist status, romance, bloodline, lore weight, future usefulness, or authorial convenience.

---

# 1. Core Invariant

No actor is immune to death merely because the actor is:

- a protagonist;
- a major character;
- a love interest;
- part of a harem or romance network;
- a spouse, fiancé, lover, child, parent, sibling, mentor, rival, or heir;
- politically important;
- strategically valuable;
- unique;
- rare;
- irreplaceable;
- part of a prophecy;
- tied to a bloodline;
- tied to a seal, artifact, institution, faction, dynasty, or state;
- needed by another actor emotionally;
- needed by another actor politically;
- needed by a later planned arc;
- narratively unfinished;
- unresolved in a relationship;
- intended to betray someone later;
- intended to reconcile later;
- intended to reveal information later;
- intended to become stronger later;
- intended to serve as a bridge between factions;
- tied to a trajectory, motif, thematic role, or planned payoff.

```text
RELATIONSHIP
!= IMMUNITY

IMPORTANCE
!= IMMUNITY

UNIQUENESS
!= IMMUNITY

PROTAGONIST STATUS
!= IMMUNITY

FUTURE USEFULNESS
!= IMMUNITY

UNFINISHED ARC
!= IMMUNITY

LORE WEIGHT
!= IMMUNITY

POLITICAL VALUE
!= IMMUNITY

BLOODLINE VALUE
!= IMMUNITY
```

If the causal state produces lethal conditions and no established mechanism prevents death, the actor may die.

---

# 2. Mortality Is A Causal Property, Not A Narrative Reward

Death must follow from causal state, not from dramatic preference.

Use:

```text
current state
-> actor information
-> actor decision
-> authority / access
-> movement / positioning
-> exposure
-> interaction
-> injury / failure / capture / survival
-> resulting state
```

Do not use:

```text
actor is important
-> survival

actor has unfinished romance
-> survival

actor must appear later
-> rescue appears

actor is needed for lore
-> lethal event becomes non-lethal

actor is loved
-> enemy hesitates without cause

actor is protagonist
-> impossible coincidence saves them
```

Mortality must remain compatible with:

- established capabilities;
- available resources;
- valid routes;
- timing;
- medical or magical support;
- actor knowledge;
- protection;
- authority;
- opponent behavior;
- local conditions;
- previously established constraints.

---

# 3. Relationship Does Not Grant Physical Protection

A relationship may change decisions.

A relationship does not automatically change physics, capability, timing, access, or hostile intent.

Valid:

```text
A loves B
-> A chooses to protect B
-> A reallocates resources
-> A takes risk
-> survival chance may change
```

Invalid:

```text
A loves B
-> B survives
```

Valid:

```text
B is politically valuable
-> guards are assigned
-> extraction plan exists
-> fallback route exists
-> survival chance changes
```

Invalid:

```text
B is politically valuable
-> B cannot die
```

The protection mechanism must exist.

Importance may motivate protection.

Importance does not replace protection.

---

# 4. Lore Importance Does Not Equal Survival Guarantee

Lore may establish that an actor is:

- the only known bearer of a trait;
- a lineage bottleneck;
- a seal bearer;
- a claimant;
- a key witness;
- a unique interface;
- a rare specialist;
- a strategic bridge;
- a high-value diplomatic node.

This establishes **consequence**, not immunity.

```text
actor is unique
-> death consequence may be severe

actor is unique
!= death impossible
```

If an actor is a genuine single point of failure:

```text
actor dies
-> system may fail
-> succession may fail
-> seal may weaken
-> faction may fracture
-> war aim may change
-> actors may panic, adapt, defect, or escalate
```

Do not silently introduce a substitute to preserve the system.

Do not silently reveal a hidden backup that was never established.

---

# 5. Protagonist Status Gives No Survival Privilege

A protagonist may:

- make better decisions;
- have unusual capabilities;
- have unusual information;
- have allies;
- have institutional value;
- have stronger preparation;
- have more narrative focus.

None of these imply guaranteed survival.

```text
PROTAGONIST
-> may possess established advantages

PROTAGONIST
!= protected outcome
```

If the protagonist enters a lethal causal chain without sufficient defense, route, information, timing, or support:

```text
death remains valid
```

The simulation must be able to continue without the protagonist.

If the world cannot continue after one protagonist dies, that dependency must come from established world structure, not from narrative necessity.

---

# 6. Romance And Harem Relations Do Not Create Plot Armor

Romantic or intimate relationships may produce:

- rescue attempts;
- information sharing;
- concealment;
- sacrifice;
- altered priorities;
- political leverage;
- hesitation;
- retaliation;
- extraction;
- defection;
- bargaining;
- revenge;
- withdrawal.

They do not automatically produce successful rescue.

```text
LOVE
-> changes incentive

LOVE
!= successful intervention

LOVE
!= perfect timing

LOVE
!= access

LOVE
!= authority

LOVE
!= combat superiority

LOVE
!= medical capacity

LOVE
!= enemy restraint
```

If one actor attempts to save another:

```text
intent
-> information path
-> authorization or unilateral action
-> route
-> timing
-> capability
-> interaction
-> result
```

Every link remains causal.

---

# 7. Bloodline, Marriage And Succession Do Not Grant Immunity

Dynastic value may increase protection.

Dynastic value may also increase danger.

A bloodline bearer may become:

- a target for assassination;
- a hostage;
- a forced-marriage target;
- a research target;
- a bargaining asset;
- a claimant to be eliminated;
- a symbol whose death changes legitimacy.

Therefore:

```text
HIGH DYNASTIC VALUE
!= SAFETY

HIGH DYNASTIC VALUE
may imply
HIGHER PROTECTION
and / or
HIGHER TARGET VALUE
```

The net effect is scenario-dependent.

Do not assume protection wins.

Do not assume threat wins.

Trace both.

---

# 8. Strategic Value Does Not Require Capture Instead Of Death

An enemy may prefer capture when capture is:

- feasible;
- authorized;
- worth the operational risk;
- compatible with war aims;
- compatible with information;
- compatible with timing;
- compatible with command intent.

Do not infer:

```text
target is valuable
-> enemy will not kill target
```

Possible valid states include:

```text
capture preferred
capture attempted
capture fails
target killed
```

or:

```text
enemy lacks knowledge of value
-> lethal action proceeds
```

or:

```text
enemy knows value
but local survival / tempo / doctrine / command constraint
-> lethal action still chosen
```

Value does not erase local conditions.

---

# 9. Unfinished Relationship Is Not A Protection Condition

A relationship may be unresolved.

That does not create a right to future closure.

The following may remain unfinished permanently:

- confession;
- reconciliation;
- revenge;
- betrayal;
- apology;
- marriage;
- alliance;
- succession;
- political bargain;
- secret reveal;
- rivalry;
- redemption;
- investigation.

```text
UNFINISHED
!= INVALID DEATH

NO PAYOFF
!= CAUSAL ERROR
```

A relationship may end because one actor dies before narrative closure.

This is not automatically subversion.

It is a valid consequence if the causal state supports it.

---

# 10. Planned Future Role Is Not Evidence

Do not preserve an actor because the actor is intended for:

- later arc;
- later war;
- later betrayal;
- later faction switch;
- later marriage;
- later reveal;
- later power growth;
- later political office;
- later succession;
- later final battle.

Future authorial intention is not in-world protection.

```text
AUTHORIAL PLAN
!= ACTOR RESOURCE

PLANNED PAYOFF
!= CAUSAL SHIELD
```

If survival is required for a future branch, that branch remains conditional on survival.

Do not reverse-engineer current events to guarantee it.

---

# 11. Relationship Networks Must Survive Actor Loss

A relationship network must not collapse automatically when one central actor dies unless the network is canonically dependent on that actor.

For a coalition, harem-like network, alliance cluster, academy cohort, or factional network:

```text
central actor dies
-> surviving actors update goals
-> trust changes
-> alliances change
-> succession occurs or fails
-> information redistributes
-> retaliation / withdrawal / defection may occur
-> network may fragment, persist, or transform
```

Do not use:

```text
central actor dies
-> everyone loses agency
-> story ends
```

unless the setting explicitly establishes such dependency.

Every major actor should retain:

- own goals;
- own factional interests;
- own survival interests;
- own relationships outside the central actor;
- own information;
- own authority;
- own capacity to adapt.

---

# 12. Death Must Propagate

Death is not a scene-local event.

It changes state.

Possible propagation:

```text
death
-> command vacancy
-> succession
-> legitimacy shift
-> intelligence loss
-> morale change
-> revenge incentive
-> treaty instability
-> route loss
-> capability loss
-> factional split
-> inheritance
-> hostage value disappears
-> alliance recalculation
-> enemy adaptation
```

Do not reset these consequences because the camera moves elsewhere.

```text
DEATH PERSISTS
```

Recovery requires an actual transition.

---

# 13. No Retroactive Protection

After a lethal event begins, do not introduce new protection merely because the outcome became undesirable.

Invalid:

```text
actor is about to die
-> reveal unknown shield
-> reveal unknown healer
-> reveal unknown teleport
-> reveal unknown hidden guard
-> reveal unknown prophecy immunity
-> reveal unknown resurrection
```

unless the mechanism was already established or explicitly introduced as a labeled hypothetical before the decisive transition.

A hidden capability may exist in canon.

But if it was not established for the current task, do not use it as emergency plot repair.

```text
UNKNOWN
!= EMERGENCY SAVE
```

---

# 14. Resurrection And Return Are Separate Mechanics

If the setting contains resurrection, rebirth, undying, soul recovery, clone, reconstruction, time reversal, or other return mechanisms:

```text
death
!= automatically permanent
```

but also:

```text
return-capable setting
!= automatic return
```

A return requires its own established mechanism:

- eligibility;
- trigger;
- cost;
- controller;
- timing;
- access;
- failure condition;
- identity continuity;
- consequence.

Do not use resurrection as hidden plot armor.

Death may still matter even when return is possible.

---

# 15. Information About Importance Is Actor-Specific

A target may be important in canon.

An enemy may not know that.

Keep separate:

```text
SIMULATION KNOWS VALUE
!= ACTOR KNOWS VALUE

ACTOR KNOWS VALUE
!= ACTOR UNDERSTANDS VALUE

ACTOR UNDERSTANDS VALUE
!= ACTOR CAN EXPLOIT VALUE
```

Do not make enemies preserve a target because the reader knows the target is important.

Do not make allies rescue a target because the simulation knows the target is in danger unless they have an information path.

---

# 16. Competence Does Not Equal Survival

A competent actor may still die because:

- information is incomplete;
- route fails;
- timing fails;
- enemy adapts;
- resource runs out;
- authority blocks action;
- ally defects;
- actor takes a deliberate risk;
- multiple failures overlap;
- local superiority is insufficient;
- an assumption was wrong;
- the opponent is also competent.

```text
COMPETENT
!= INVULNERABLE
```

Competence changes the causal path.

It does not guarantee the result.

---

# 17. Ruthlessness Does Not Equal Survival

A ruthless actor may:

- sacrifice others;
- retreat earlier;
- defect;
- abandon an objective;
- kill hostages;
- use deception;
- preserve self-interest aggressively.

This may improve survival.

It may also create enemies, miscalculation, isolation, retaliation, or overconfidence.

```text
RUTHLESS
!= IMMUNE
```

---

# 18. Sacrifice Must Be Real

If an actor sacrifices themselves:

```text
sacrifice
-> risk or death must remain real
```

Do not convert sacrifice into guaranteed survival because the act is emotionally important.

If rescue occurs, it needs a valid causal path.

A sacrifice that accidentally survives may be valid.

A sacrifice that survives because the story "cannot lose them" is not.

---

# 19. Betrayal Does Not Require Survival To Pay Off

An actor may die:

- before betrayal;
- during betrayal;
- immediately after betrayal;
- before revealing motive;
- before switching sides publicly.

Do not preserve them only so the betrayal can complete.

Other actors may never learn the full truth.

```text
INTENT
!= COMPLETED ACTION

PLANNED BETRAYAL
!= SURVIVAL RIGHT
```

---

# 20. Capture, Injury And Disability Are Not Mandatory Substitutes For Death

Do not avoid death by automatically converting lethal outcomes into:

- capture;
- coma;
- permanent injury;
- missing status;
- miraculous survival;
- off-screen disappearance.

These are valid only if the causal state supports them.

```text
DEATH WOULD BE INCONVENIENT
!= CAPTURE SHOULD HAPPEN
```

Likewise, do not force death when capture or survival is more causally supported.

This overlay removes immunity.

It does not create a death bias.

---

# 21. Death Probability Must Not Be Faked

Do not assign numeric death probability without a model or user-authorized assumptions.

Use qualitative causal assessment when sufficient:

```text
LOW EXPOSURE
MODERATE RISK
SEVERE RISK
LETHAL STATE
SURVIVAL PATH ESTABLISHED
SURVIVAL PATH UNKNOWN
```

These are analysis labels, not canon terminology.

No random roll is required unless the user explicitly asks for stochastic simulation.

# 22. Total War Interaction

In Total War RP, mortality interacts with:

- mobility;
- supply;
- command;
- information;
- replacement;
- sustainment;
- medical support;
- magical support;
- extraction;
- territory;
- airspace;
- diplomacy;
- factional politics.

Use the Total War rule:

```text
HAS CAPABILITY
!= AVAILABLE NOW
!= DEPLOYABLE HERE
!= AUTHORIZED
!= SUSTAINABLE
```

A rescue asset that exists elsewhere does not automatically save the actor.

A healer who exists does not help without access and timing.

A faction that values the actor does not automatically have extraction capability.

# 23. No Relationship-Based Enemy Incompetence

Do not weaken an enemy because the target is loved by a protagonist.

Invalid examples:

```text
enemy forgets to confirm kill
enemy stops pursuit without reason
enemy reveals plan
enemy leaves escape route open
enemy misses obvious shot
enemy delays because target has unresolved romance
```

Enemy behavior must follow:

- information;
- goals;
- doctrine;
- incentives;
- constraints;
- competence;
- local risk.

If hesitation exists because of personal relation, politics, orders, hostage value, or uncertainty, establish that path.

---

# 24. No Ally Omniscience

Do not make allies arrive because the actor is important.

Rescue requires:

```text
danger
-> information path
-> interpretation
-> decision
-> mobilization
-> route
-> timing
-> intervention
```

If any decisive link fails, rescue may fail.

---

# 25. Single Point Of Failure Is Allowed

Do not redesign away every vulnerable actor merely to keep the story safe.

If canon establishes:

```text
Actor X
= true single point of failure
```

then:

```text
X dies
-> catastrophic consequence may occur
```

That is not a reason to grant X immunity.

It is a reason for actors to:

- protect X;
- create redundancy if possible;
- control access;
- create succession;
- seek substitutes;
- reduce dependency.

If no substitute exists, the risk remains real.

---

# 26. Relationship-Based Protection Must Have Cost

When one actor protects another because of relationship:

possible costs include:

- lost position;
- lost time;
- exposed route;
- weakened front;
- political cost;
- command conflict;
- resource expenditure;
- reputation;
- intelligence leak;
- retaliation;
- opportunity cost.

Do not treat protection as free.

```text
PROTECTION
-> COST / TRADE-OFF
```

If protection is costless because a real established capability makes it costless, use that capability.

Do not make it costless because the relationship is important.

---

# 27. Death Can Revalue Every Relationship

When an actor dies, surviving relationships may be reinterpreted.

Possible states:

```text
love -> revenge
love -> withdrawal
love -> political inheritance
rivalry -> regret
alliance -> collapse
betrayal suspicion -> unresolved forever
engagement -> succession crisis
mentor relation -> doctrine split
harem / coalition -> factional fragmentation
```

Do not preselect one emotional consequence.

Actors respond according to their own goals, information, beliefs, obligations, and incentives.

---

# 28. Actor Autonomy After Central Death

If MC1, MC2, MC3, MC4, a major love interest, faction leader, heir, or strategic node dies:

the surviving world remains active.

For each surviving major actor:

```text
GOAL
INFORMATION
AUTHORITY
RESOURCE
RELATIONSHIP
RISK
AVAILABLE ACTION
```

must be reevaluated.

Do not make survivors exist only as grief reactions.

They retain independent political and strategic agency.

# 29. Canon And Simulation Boundary

This overlay does not state that any current AetherFire actor will die.

It states only:

```text
NO ACTOR RECEIVES MORTALITY IMMUNITY
FROM RELATIONSHIP, ROLE, LORE IMPORTANCE,
OR NARRATIVE EXPECTATION.
```

A death in simulation remains:

```text
HYPOTHETICAL
```

unless explicitly confirmed into canon by the user.

A survived branch also does not become canon automatically.

# 30. Anti-Plot-Convenience Checks

Before resolving a lethal scene or operation, check:

1. Is the actor surviving because of an established mechanism?
2. Is the rescue path supported by information, route, timing, authority, and capability?
3. Did relationship importance silently become physical immunity?
4. Did future narrative usefulness influence the result?
5. Did the enemy become incompetent only to preserve the actor?
6. Did an ally gain information they could not have?
7. Was an UNKNOWN converted into an emergency save?
8. Was capture chosen only because death would be inconvenient?
9. Was injury chosen only to preserve a later role?
10. Would the same causal state kill a less important actor?

If the answer to 3–10 is yes, re-evaluate the transition.

The test is:

```text
SAME CAUSAL STATE
-> SAME MORTALITY RULES
```

not:

```text
SAME SCREEN TIME
-> SAME OUTCOME
```

# 31. Compact Decision Rule

For any potentially lethal event:

```text
1. ESTABLISH EXPOSURE
2. ESTABLISH INFORMATION
3. ESTABLISH AVAILABLE PROTECTION
4. ESTABLISH TIMING
5. ESTABLISH INTERACTION
6. APPLY CONSEQUENCE
7. PROPAGATE LOSS
```

Do not insert:

```text
8. CHECK WHETHER CHARACTER IS TOO IMPORTANT TO DIE
```

That step does not exist.
